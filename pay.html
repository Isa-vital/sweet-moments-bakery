Payment verification

A future operating business would verify payment through its own mobile money, bank or licensed payment-provider records. A customer screenshot alone should not be accepted as proof of cleared payment.

Proposed refunds

Eligibility would depend on the order status and circumstances. Personalised items already in production may not qualify unless faulty or not as agreed. Approved refunds would use an agreed traceable method.

Proposed delivery

Delivery is planned for Kampala and surrounding areas. Availability, fees and timing would be confirmed from the location, product, quantity, date and route. No delivery is booked by this site.

Why these methods

Familiar choices for individual and business customers.

Mobile money is widely accessible for proposed everyday orders, while bank transfer provides a traceable option for offices, schools, cafés and other business buyers. Cash on delivery may help customers who prefer to pay at handover, but it would only be offered after the bakery confirms the product, location, delivery fee and order value.

These choices are provisional. Before a real launch, the business would compare them with customer research, operational costs and the requirements of licensed payment providers.

Proposed policy

Cryptocurrency would not be accepted at launch.

It is less familiar to the bakery's intended customers, can create price and consumer-protection risks, and is unnecessary when regulated payment channels are available. Bank of Uganda recognises possible innovation from virtual assets but remains cautious while a regulatory framework is being developed.
